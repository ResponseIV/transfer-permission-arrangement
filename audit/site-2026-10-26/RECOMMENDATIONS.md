## Choose one editorial direction

Two directions are coherent with the evidence. Combining both in full would recreate the current problem of too many parallel ways into the same argument.

### A. The case notebook — recommended

**Promise:** follow one apparent exchange as each record changes what can be concluded. The publication becomes recognizable through its handling of evidence.

Keep the current question and opening. Carry a small, consistent convention through the chapters: **record available → claim supported → alternative still open**. Use the actual scripted-control excerpt as a fully disclosed reconstruction; use an observed arrangement and planted leak as its counterexamples. The main narrative chooses the cases; the desk retains all eight for inspection. Complete logs remain available, with any condensed comparison marked as such.

Three distinct tasks:

- **Essentials:** understand the three questions through a short case and one counterexample. Keep the main limits and paper route.
- **Investigation:** test an interpretation against archive, permission, leak and script controls; show one concrete failed shortcut.
- **Research:** inspect operational labels, coding, populations, registration/validation and reproduction via a concise evidence map.

**Tradeoff:** strongest alignment with the existing interaction and owner's educational aim; requires careful annotation and a better phone comparison. It could become a gimmicky detective game if answers are framed as a trick. Avoid scoring the reader or pretending that hidden instructions can be deduced from pages alone.

**Creative acceptance:** an unfamiliar reader can point to the exact added record that changes their conclusion, and explain one thing still unresolved. They should remember a real case and its limitation, not only the three headings. Subject-specific annotations recur across article and desk without introducing a new decorative vocabulary.

### B. How the interpretation survived — an authored research essay

**Promise:** follow the research decisions and failed expectations from the first detector result to the scripted-control challenge.

Use a primarily linear essay: what the initial result appeared to establish; why the archive and negative mix complicated it; what the Sol follow-up showed; why the supplied sequence limited the arrangement inference. Keep two or three inline exhibits and make the desk an optional appendix. Use first person only where the source supports the author's actual decision, and distinguish prior work, registered follow-ups and retrospective diagnostics.

**Tradeoff:** stronger voice and narrative momentum, less interface burden and easier phone reading. It asks for more substantial prose editing and makes the independent evidence-inspection feature less central. The three depths must become short summary / full narrative / annotated methods, not three partly duplicated essays.

**Creative acceptance:** every major transition is caused by a specific result or unresolved alternative. A reader can recount why the next test was needed. No claim of historical motivation is inferred merely from the order in which material appears in the paper.

**Recommendation:** choose A. The existing real-record reveal is the site's best original asset. Extend it with purposeful annotations and counterexamples, while borrowing B's specific research reversals for the investigation depth. Do not build both a long detective exercise and a complete process memoir.
