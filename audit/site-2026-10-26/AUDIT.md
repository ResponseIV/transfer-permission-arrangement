# Independent website audit — 26 October 2026

**Verdict: a worthwhile research companion, with a specific teaching idea, that is still more polished than fully authored. Overall: 6/10.** It is instructive enough to be useful. It is not yet an exceptional editorial or educational publication. A further round of font changes, quieter buttons or decorative animation would not resolve the gap.

The distinction between transfer, permission and arrangement is a real intellectual point of view. The opening makes it tangible by adding evidence to an apparent exchange. The paper's uncomfortable qualifications survive into the site. Those are substantial strengths. The middle of the article, however, often restates the thesis instead of developing the investigation; the evidence desk exposes controls and logs before it establishes a clear learning task. The three depths contain genuinely different substance, but switching can lose the reader's chapter. Accessibility also breaks at a particularly important boundary: distinguishing observer evidence from fixture truth.

This judgment concerns the effect of the work, not whether a particular author or model produced it. A serif font, an em dash or a familiar layout is not evidence of poor authorship. The strongest evidence of a generic treatment is the gap between the specificity of the experiment and the generality of some of its editorial presentation.

## Scoring rubric

Scores are critical judgments, not measurements or probabilities. **2** means a serious weakness; **5** means a competent but ordinary execution; **9** means exceptional, purposeful work. Intermediate integers describe the distance between those anchors. Ten would require unusually complete execution and strong evidence that the intended readers succeed. Confidence describes the evidence behind the judgment, not score precision.

| Criterion / what is assessed | Low: 2 | Competent: 5 | Exceptional: 9 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Uniqueness / originality: dependence of form on this subject | Subject can be swapped wholesale | Specific content in familiar furniture | Research determines a recognizable reading form |
| Creative quality: invention that helps understanding | Ornament substitutes for explanation | Sound familiar devices | A restrained original device reveals something difficult |
| Sustained interest: reward for continuing | Opening exhausts the story | Useful sequence with some repetition | Each section changes the reader's model or raises a productive question |
| Educational clarity: concepts and inference | Jargon or misleading simplification | Main argument understandable | Novices can explain the distinction and apply it to a new case |
| Narrative / information architecture: sequence and orientation | Readers cannot locate the argument | Logical chapters and usable navigation | Different arrivals and continuations feel intentional and effortless |
| Layout / typography: hierarchy, measure, spacing, density | Reading is physically difficult | Legible and consistent | Optical craft supports both close reading and scanning at every size |
| Prose / voice: specificity, rhythm, honesty | Generic claims and opaque sentences | Clear but uneven | A particular mind explains particular evidence economically |
| Depth differentiation: value of the three journeys | Same copy with different labels | Useful detail levels | Distinct guided learning tasks with continuity and no needless repetition |
| Scientific fidelity: accuracy and provenance relative to submission | Claims exceed records | Core claims correct, caveats recoverable | Exact scope, uncertainty and source boundaries travel with every claim |
| Explanatory interaction: learning gained from action | Clicking gates static content | Helpful reveal and inspection | Reader can test an inference and understand why it changes |
| Accessibility / responsiveness: equivalent access and resilient layout | Key tasks inaccessible | Core controls/layout usable with gaps | Equivalent reasoning, robust reflow and verified assistive use |
| Craft / coherence: one publication from entrance to source | Disconnected pages and inconsistent language | Consistent visual system and source handoff | Voice, navigation, evidence and paper reinforce one another throughout |

**Overall 6/10 is not an arithmetic average.** The intended purpose is an interesting, accessible, distinctive route into the paper. Narrative progression, learning interaction and continuity therefore matter more than code cleanliness or the correctness of a type scale. Fidelity and basic legibility are strong prerequisites; they do not compensate for the desk's weak learning sequence or make an ordinary visual grammar exceptional. Excellence would require readers to experience the research's reversals, keep their place and successfully reason from limited records to a carefully scoped conclusion.

## Prioritized findings

Severity: **P1** = substantial failure in a central task; **P2** = material loss of comprehension, interest or usability; **P3** = limited polish or resilience issue. “Demonstrated” means observed behavior or directly inspected structure. “Judgment” means an editorial/design assessment. None is a request to redesign during this audit.

### F1 — P1: Changing depth can abandon the current chapter

**Demonstrated; high confidence.** `docs/assets/site.js:28–61`, especially candidate selection at `32–38`. At 1440×1000, load `/?level=beginner#transfer`, then select **The investigation** using its visible label. The Transfer heading moves from **y=183 to y=4,221**, while scrollY changes from **2,369 to 1,343**. The URL still reads `level=amateur#transfer`; the viewport shows the setup diagram. The displaced position remained after a separate subsequent check, so this was not a transient capture.

Evidence: [displaced chapter](evidence/depth-switch-transfer-place-1440.jpg). A large earlier container whose bottom extends into the viewport can be chosen as the anchor even though its top is far above it. Preserving that container's top does not preserve the reader's current heading; this is the likely mechanism, inferred from the source.

**Impact:** the feature intended to adapt an explanation forces rereading or manual recovery. **Remedy:** anchor the current visible chapter/heading, with a deliberate fallback when a section disappears; do not anchor an offscreen container's start. Verify switching every pair of depths at several early and late chapters, at desktop and phone widths. Keep the active chapter in view, with no large unexpected displacement.

### F2 — P1: Observer evidence and fixture truth lose their names in desktop accessibility output

**Demonstrated structure; high confidence.** `docs/explorer.html:117` sets the visible column headings `aria-hidden="true"`. `docs/assets/style.css:586` hides each repeated `.mobile-label` at 760px and above. `docs/assets/explorer.js:103–152,274–278` creates unlabeled pane divs. The [captured accessibility tree](evidence/desktop-accessibility-tree.txt) presents the anonymous observer read followed by the true actor's read, without announcing the distinction.

Evidence: [desktop lanes after scrolling](evidence/desktop-observer-truth-labels-1440.jpg). Sighted readers also lose the original column headings as they scroll, while record controls stay sticky. The screenshot illustrates that visual context loss; the text capture establishes the accessibility structure. No claim is made about an actual screen reader's exact speech.

**Impact:** users can confuse what an observer knew with what the experiment recorded, precisely the distinction this publication teaches. **Remedy:** programmatically name each pane and preserve its identity while scrolling; use accessible heading associations or appropriately structured row/column semantics. Check single-case and revealed comparison views with a desktop screen reader. Visual and spoken reading must both distinguish record from reconstruction.

### F3 — P2: The evidence desk is an inspector before it is a guided explanation

**Demonstrated layout/state plus educational judgment; high/medium confidence respectively.** `docs/explorer.html:57–112`; `docs/assets/explorer.js:267–281,310–331`; `.ex-head`, `.ex-summary` and `.compare` styles. At 1440×1000 the [comparison arrival](evidence/desktop-compare-arrival-1440.jpg) reaches the record controls at the lower edge; actual logs start below the first screen. Introductory instructions, a start recommendation, view mode, case choice and another question box precede them. In single-case mode the summary already identifies the arrangement and narrates its completion before the reader steps through it.

At 375px the [comparison is consecutive logs](evidence/phone-comparison-sequential-375.jpg): Episode A is about **871px** high before reveal, followed by a roughly **925px** Episode B. The mechanism avoids horizontal overflow, but “Compare two” no longer provides a simultaneous comparison. On desktop the two lists also have different event counts and unaligned semantic milestones ([logs](evidence/desktop-compare-records-1440.jpg)). Equal row numbers are not equivalent actions.

**Impact:** a first-time reader manages interface choices and memory before making an inference; an already-solved case reduces discovery to replay. **Remedy:** give the recommended pair one explicit question and an immediate evidence area. In a learning route, withhold the explanatory answer until requested. Keep the complete record as an inspectable route. Compare **offer → read → reply → use → instructions**, marking absent milestones rather than implying the traces are identical. On phones show those milestones in adjacent A/B groups. Do not fabricate alignment or remove real intervening events from the complete logs.

### F4 — P2: Attribution and intent boundaries need to travel with the example

**Demonstrated wording; medium confidence about misreading risk.** `docs/index.html:76–97` names **Alpha writes / Beta replies** under “Sol / event log.” The caption discloses condensation and actors identified by the experiment; it is not an invented quotation. But it does not directly say the observer's shared-board authors are anonymous, or identify the case as the scripted control before the reveal. `docs/data/episodes.json:418,428,1270,3023` includes beginner phrases such as “the agents arranged it themselves,” “nobody arranged it,” and “Alpha just gave it away.”

Evidence: [initial exhibit](evidence/desktop-opening-1440.jpg), [instruction reveal](evidence/desktop-opening-instructions-1440.jpg); the source case is `sol-scripted-01`. The paper's scope at `paper/report.md:324–327` says the definition does **not** establish intention, and that the leak is fixture-planted with no downstream harm.

**Impact:** a skim can mistake reconstructed actors for observer knowledge, or an event classification for a finding about intent. **Remedy:** source line: “Condensed scripted-control record. Alpha/Beta identify the experiment's recorded actors; shared-board authors were hidden from the observer.” If preserving a surprise is essential, an alternative is to disclose reconstruction immediately and label the control explicitly at the reveal. Prefer “no offer was recorded” to “nobody arranged it,” and “Alpha wrote the code; Beta read and used it” to “gave it away.” Preserve the puzzle and the real excerpts.

### F5 — P2: The investigation describes good research instead of letting readers experience its reversals

**Editorial judgment; medium–high confidence.** `docs/index.html:346–364` promises “Build the explanation. Then try to break it.” Its steps include “Fix the measuring tools first,” “Construct the look-alikes” and “Challenge the strongest remaining clue.” They are true, but could describe many studies. Failed expectations are sent to an external analysis link. `:715` introduces “the test that matters most,” ranking the work instead of showing why the test matters.

**Impact:** the site supplies conclusions and process assurances, while the curiosity and the author's decisions remain mostly in the paper. **Remedy:** use one specific reversal: a later retrospective flow check found only **one of eight** Sol follow-up transfers and missed seven encoded carriers (`paper/report.md:741–746`). Explicitly call it retrospective; it did not cause an earlier registration. Develop the scripted-control challenge as prediction → result → changed inference. Explain why the seven one-way transfers and first-mover asymmetry matter (`paper/report.md:204–210`), without claiming a general model tendency. Replace an abstract process list rather than adding another chapter of signposting.

### F6 — P2: Repeated rhetorical furniture and shifting metaphors flatten the voice

**Editorial judgment, not proof of machine authorship; medium confidence.** The opening, central finding and chapter heads repeatedly use compressed contrast/question structures: “A secret moved. Was there a deal?” (`docs/index.html:63`), “Sharing a secret isn’t the whole story.” (`:102`), “A code on a page isn’t a code received.” (`:427`), “The same exchange. A different rule.” (`:615`), “An offer and a reply. But whose plan?” (`:668`). Most express real distinctions. The excess is local repetition: transfer result paragraph → three numeric tiles → caution → false-alarm note (`:434–485`); arrangement explanation → comparison link → figure → further comparison (`:671–798`).

The setup uses a notebook, the shared-source account a filing cabinet, and beginner records a visitors' book/sheets. “We,” “the researchers” and “the author” shift narrator. The beginner explorer's visitors' book also becomes the article's access log at the next depth. These choices add translation without adding understanding.

**Impact:** rhythm becomes predictable and the research feels depersonalized. **Remedy:** keep the strongest contrasts and their evidence; remove duplicate narration within a finding. Use “access log” after one plain definition, and choose a consistent narrator for the single-author study. Concrete before/after suggestions are in [JOURNEYS-AND-PROSE.md](JOURNEYS-AND-PROSE.md). “The judge called Astra found…” is grammatical, but a garden-path phrase; simplify it, do not call it a factual or grammatical failure.

### F7 — P2: Competent typographic craft does not yet establish a distinctive publication

**Design judgment; medium confidence.** `docs/assets/style.css:225–283,286–350,689–766`. The desktop opening, 176px rail, 64px gap and 656px prose column are orderly; measured body text is 18px with 30.24px leading. The masthead is one line at 1440 and 768; at 375 it becomes `T/P/A`. The depth choices are understated underlined labels. These address the stated complaints in actual renderings ([desktop](evidence/desktop-opening-1440.jpg), [tablet](evidence/tablet-opening-768.jpg), [phone](evidence/phone-opening-375.jpg)). The initial exhibit fits with the question on desktop; on tablet it follows the hero, leaving some unused width because it remains capped at 36rem. This is an optical balance tradeoff, not a broken breakpoint.

The subject-swap test is mixed. Blue rules, sans masthead, serif body, italic accented question, arrows, large-number strips and a paper-cover mockup could serve almost any research explainer. The evidence plate, anonymous-versus-attributed records and encoded strings resist that swap. They supply the identity; the publication furniture mostly does not. The boxed explorer, rounded carrier tags and verdict panels reintroduce a utility-dashboard feel, though several boxes serve a legitimate grouping function.

**Impact:** the cover looks considered, but sustained reading is less recognizably this project. **Remedy:** develop one recurring subject-specific explanatory convention—record/reconstruction/claim annotations attached to real events—rather than more visual motifs. Retain the type and reading measure unless an actual problem emerges. The 8px grid is a useful framework, not literally universal: source uses 10/14/18/22/26/38px values and continuous chapter padding. This is a consistency opportunity, not a finding that every nonmultiple is visually wrong. Consolidate spacing during a relevant edit and judge optical alignment afterward.

### F8 — P2: The levels differ in substance but not enough in their learning tasks

**Source comparison plus editorial judgment; high/medium confidence.** `docs/index.html:142–177,258–418,500–610,907–1079`. Essentials omits the look-alike/process/record/matching chapters; investigation adds them and comparative results; research adds the operational label, detailed tables, statistics, chronology and reproduction. These are meaningful differences, not three cosmetic rewrites. The default is useful and self-contained.

The specialist route largely expands the same spine into an appendix. It needs a compact map of what is frozen, post-hoc, judged and hand-coded before the tables. The investigation's base-rate paragraph at `:501–504` compresses 1%, 206, 19%, 11%, 100%, 35% and 5% into one small argument; the chart then repeats it in a long caption and another reading note ([phone chart](evidence/phone-base-rate-chart-375.jpg)). A log axis and interval bounds are not taught by calling the reader intermediate.

**Impact:** the essentials sometimes hides the most interesting reasoning, while added detail can feel like a burden rather than a new journey. **Remedy:** retain one concrete alternative in essentials, frame investigation around counterexamples, and begin research with the label/record/validation map. Teach one base-rate scenario with a simple, clearly hypothetical population before showing all curves. Distinguish an AI transfer judge from human event coding at first use. Fix F1 before encouraging switching.

### F9 — P2: Direct explorer arrivals lack a compact setup and consistent orientation

**Demonstrated copy/layout; high confidence.** `docs/explorer.html:60–69` explains controls before explaining the synthetic two-agent wiki task. Its “left/right” description is wrong for the stacked phone layout. Case summaries name codes and rules but presume familiarity with the task and fixture. The [phone arrival](evidence/phone-explorer-arrival-375.jpg) shows the fixed stepper before any event is visible.

**Impact:** a shared inner link is a weaker entrance than the home page; terms such as “harm without agreement” can sound broader than this controlled task. **Remedy:** open with one compact setup sentence and the exact current question. Use “observer record / experiment's complete record,” not spatial directions. Show the fixed stepper when the event area is being read, or give its initial state a clear connection to the evidence below. Preserve a nearby link to the article's relevant chapter, not only its top.

### F10 — P2: The URL identifies the case but cannot reproduce the evidence state

**Demonstrated; high confidence.** `docs/assets/explorer.js:284–307,351–411`. At `#single/sol-21`, select **+ Instructions** and advance to **Step 3 of 9**. Reload the unchanged URL: it returns to **+ Access log**, **Step 1 of 9**. Record and step changes do not enter the URL; case/mode changes do. Back/Forward restored the tested comparison routes correctly.

**Impact:** a reader cannot cite the exact limited record that supports an inference or return to it later. **Remedy:** represent record, step and reveal state in a stable share link, using replaceState for frequent steps if desired. This need not pollute browser history. Keep existing case fragments compatible and test copied links in a fresh page.

### F11 — P3: Print and failure/fallback behavior need completion before claiming robustness

**Source-derived risks; medium confidence, not rendered failures.** `docs/assets/style.css:584,657–660,769–775` keeps future events `display:none` and hides explorer controls for print. Printing at Step 1 appears liable to produce only that step without a visible way to reveal the rest. `docs/explorer.html:72–76` provides a raw JSON no-JS handoff, not an accessible static evidence account. `docs/assets/explorer.js:414–437` has a fetch-error message, but no pending loading status; even hosted failures receive local Python-server instructions.

**Impact:** reading outside the normal browser flow is less complete than the article's main path. **Remedy:** print the full selected case with named panes and provenance; supply a short static case explanation for no-JS readers; show a pending state and reader-facing recovery for hosted failures, with local setup details only where relevant. Verify these in actual browser print/no-JS/failure conditions. Do not claim a WCAG failure solely from this source inspection.

## Strengths to preserve

1. **The scientific separation is clear and useful.** Transfer, permission and arrangement require different records. The article does not collapse them into a headline about agents secretly conspiring.
2. **Numbers retain their populations.** 242/244 prescribed transfers, 0/194 clean-negative alarms, nine alarms among 12 failed prescribed exchanges, 20/20 scripted controls, 60/60 planted leaks, 39/40 scorable Sol attempts with eight transfers, and partial 24/38 and 4/20 human checks match the source. See `paper/report.md:14–20,137–225,674–688` and the corresponding article chapters.
3. **The opening's action changes the claim.** Adding reads and then instructions genuinely changes what a reader can infer; it is not animation as spectacle.
4. **The ordinary reading surface is good.** Useful measure, leading, restrained rules, native disclosure/select/radio controls and visible keyboard focus. The phone stepper kept tested events 2, 3 and 9 above its fixed bottom area, including a long offer.
5. **The source handoff is substantive.** Local submitted PDF, readable paper, code/data, citation and explicit post-submission website status. Both PDF copies hash identically; `paper/report.md` matches the submission tag. [Rendered handoff](evidence/desktop-paper-handoff-1440.jpg).
6. **Static hosting is uncomplicated.** Relative runtime assets, self-hosted fonts, no build dependency or runtime CDN. No reason was found to add a framework or visual-effects library.

The next work should preserve those foundations and make the existing research more legible and memorable. [RECOMMENDATIONS.md](RECOMMENDATIONS.md) ranks the changes and gives two coherent editorial directions.
